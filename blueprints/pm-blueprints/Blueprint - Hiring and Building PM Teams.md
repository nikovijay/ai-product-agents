# Blueprint: Hiring & Building PM Teams

> Extended Competency 25 | Source: Lenny's Newsletter & Podcast

## What This Skill Means

Hiring and building PM teams is the discipline of recruiting, evaluating, onboarding, and developing product managers who can deliver outsized impact. It goes beyond writing job descriptions and conducting interviews -- it means understanding what skills actually predict PM success at your specific company, designing interview processes that surface real signal rather than performance, building a team culture that attracts top talent, and creating career development structures that retain your best people. As a PM leader, this may be the single highest-leverage activity you perform, because the quality of your PMs determines the quality of every product decision downstream.

This competency encompasses defining what "great" looks like for a PM at your company (which varies dramatically across organizations), building interview processes that reliably identify those qualities, creating onboarding programs that get new PMs to full productivity quickly, designing career ladders that clarify expectations at each level, and fostering a PM culture of learning, mentorship, and continuous growth. It also means knowing when to hire your first PM, when to promote from within versus hiring externally, and how to build diverse teams that bring different perspectives to product decisions.

At its most advanced, this skill means you can build a PM organization from scratch -- defining the role, the career ladder, the interview process, the team structure, and the cultural norms -- in a way that consistently produces high-performing product teams and becomes a recruiting advantage in itself.

## Why It Matters in 2026

In 2026, the PM role is being redefined by AI. The foundational PM skills -- leadership, execution, strategy, communication -- remain essential, but the specific competencies within each have shifted. PMs now need fluency with AI tools, the ability to evaluate and iterate on AI-powered features, and comfort with a dramatically faster pace of iteration. Hiring processes that were calibrated for the pre-AI world may be selecting for the wrong attributes.

At the same time, the supply of aspiring PMs has never been higher, while the demand for truly excellent PMs remains intense. The challenge is not finding candidates -- it is reliably distinguishing between those who can talk about product management and those who can actually do it. Companies like Linear, Automattic, and PostHog have pioneered work trials that provide dramatically more signal than traditional interviews, and these approaches are becoming the new standard for organizations serious about hiring quality. The PM leaders who thrive in 2026 are those who treat their hiring process with the same rigor they apply to their product -- continuously testing, measuring, and iterating.

## Key Insights from Lenny's Newsletter & Podcast

1. **The top 10 PM skills, based on 20+ career ladders, are: leadership, execution, strategy, communication, delivering impact, leveraging data, planning, collaboration, vision, and technical skills.** Leadership was the single most cited skill (85% of companies), followed by execution and strategy (both 75%). Surprisingly, vision was highlighted by only 40% of companies.
   *Source: skills-pms-need-to-build.md*

2. **Work trials make it "obvious who to hire" -- and frequently surprise you.** James Hawkins (CEO of PostHog) reports that candidate performance in work trials "is frequently surprising relative to what we thought in the interviews about their skills." Linear's Karri Saarinen adds that "a conventional interview process doesn't account for whether someone is truly a builder, has good taste and judgment, can take initiative, and approaches problems productively."
   *Source: adding-a-work-trial-to-your-interview-process.md*

3. **Your first PM hire should complement the founding team's weaknesses, not duplicate their strengths.** Guy Podjarny (CEO of Snyk) hired a PM as one of his first employees because he identified the problem as "a product problem, not a tech problem." Rick Song (CEO of Persona) hired a PM first because "we knew we needed someone who could anchor our product operations and knowledge base, synthesizing insights from chaos."
   *Source: hiring-your-early-team.md*

4. **Over two-thirds of B2B companies hired an engineer as employee number one, but the exceptions are instructive.** Vanta hired a compliance expert ("We could build, but we wanted the check on are we building the right thing"). Coda hired a recruiter on the principle that "people like Kenny don't come along often." Segment and Amplitude hired customer success as employee number one. The pattern: hire to de-risk or un-bottleneck your most pressing constraint.
   *Source: hiring-your-early-team.md*

5. **Cold outreach on LinkedIn and GitHub is the second most effective channel for finding early employees.** Beyond the obvious strategy of hiring friends and former colleagues, many successful founders found great hires by reaching out to people with public contributions on GitHub or strong LinkedIn profiles -- people who had "solid contributions in open-source land, paid attention to abstractions, and were really productive."
   *Source: hiring-your-early-team.md*

6. **Job postings are a wildly underinvested recruiting asset.** Lenny argues that founders spend as much time on their sign-up flow as they should on job postings but rarely do. The best job postings tell a story, give a clear sense of company values, include transparency on salary and process, and explain not just who the role is for but who it is not for.
   *Source: examples-of-great-job-postings.md*

7. **Linear invested in an in-house recruiting function at 50 people because external agencies optimize for speed, not quality.** Jori Lallo explains: "Incentives are often tied to closed hires, so recruiters are incentivized to make hires as fast as possible. This is good for growing the team fast but hard when you want to hire only the best talent." Linear built a four-person recruiting function specifically because the best people are "hard and slow to hire."
   *Source: hiring-your-early-team.md*

8. **37signals keeps work trial prompts intentionally vague to test judgment.** Jason Fried says: "We don't want to be super-specific because I don't want to tell anyone what to do. This is all about choice. I want to see how people choose to spend their time and what they think is important to focus on." The ambiguity is the signal.
   *Source: adding-a-work-trial-to-your-interview-process.md*

## How to Build This Skill

**Foundations:**
- Study your company's PM career ladder (or build one if it does not exist). Analyze the 10 core PM skills from Lenny's research and determine how they are weighted at your organization. Use this as the foundation for your interview rubric.
- Design an interview process with clear evaluation criteria for each stage. Map each interview to a specific competency you are testing. Eliminate redundant stages that test the same thing.
- Build a "what great looks like" document for each PM level at your company, using concrete examples of past work, not abstract descriptions.

**Intermediate practices:**
- Implement a work trial for PM candidates. Start with a representative (not actual) project that is intentionally too large to complete in the allotted time, to test prioritization and judgment. Have the candidate present their work and reasoning to the hiring team.
- Create a structured onboarding program for new PMs: Week 1 is immersion in user research and data. Week 2-3 is shadowing existing PMs and attending key meetings. Week 4 is a small, scoped project that delivers real value. Month 2 is ownership of a meaningful initiative.
- Build a PM team meeting cadence that includes regular product reviews, experiment read-outs, and peer feedback sessions. These rituals create shared standards and accelerate learning.

**Advanced practices:**
- Design a PM mentorship program that pairs senior PMs with junior PMs across teams (not within the same team). Cross-team mentorship builds organizational knowledge and prevents silos.
- Create a PM hiring brand by having your PMs write publicly about their work, frameworks, and learnings. This attracts candidates who are already aligned with your approach.
- Build a calibration process for PM promotions that is transparent and data-driven. Use the action plan approach: identify 2-3 specific gaps, create concrete actions, and review monthly.

## Self-Assessment Questions

1. Can you articulate the three most important attributes that predict PM success at your specific company, and does your interview process reliably test for them?
2. Do you have a structured work trial or project-based evaluation in your PM interview process, or are you relying primarily on behavioral interviews?
3. How long does it take a new PM to reach full productivity at your company, and do you have a deliberate onboarding program to accelerate this?
4. Can you name the top skill gap on your current PM team, and do you have a plan to address it through hiring, development, or both?
5. When was the last time you systematically reviewed your PM hiring process -- measuring offer acceptance rates, new hire performance at 6 months, and interviewer calibration?
