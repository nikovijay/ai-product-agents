# Blueprint: B2B & Enterprise Product Strategy

> Extended Competency 24 | Source: Lenny's Newsletter & Podcast

## What This Skill Means

B2B and enterprise product strategy is the discipline of building products that are purchased through organizational buying processes rather than individual consumer decisions. It means understanding that your "user" and your "buyer" are often different people, that purchasing decisions involve procurement teams, security reviews, and multi-stakeholder sign-offs, and that the path from first touch to signed contract can take months or years. A PM with this skill can navigate the tension between building for the end user who loves the product and satisfying the enterprise buyer who signs the check.

This competency encompasses identifying your ideal customer profile (ICP), designing products that can expand from individual adoption to team and company-wide deployment, aligning product development with sales cycles, managing feature requests from large accounts without losing product vision, and building pricing and packaging that supports land-and-expand motion. It also means understanding when and how to move upmarket -- from SMBs to mid-market to enterprise -- and the organizational changes that transition demands.

At the highest level, B2B product strategy means you can construct a go-to-market motion that matches your product's complexity and price point to the right customer segment, build a sales pitch rooted in differentiated positioning rather than feature walkthroughs, and design a product experience that generates product-qualified leads for your sales team while also serving self-serve customers. You understand that B2B product-market fit is not binary but a continual process of finding fit with progressively larger market segments.

## Why It Matters in 2026

In 2026, the B2B landscape has been transformed by AI. Enterprise buyers are simultaneously more sophisticated in their evaluation processes and more overwhelmed by the proliferation of AI-powered tools competing for budget. The result is that "no decision" -- the buyer's inability to confidently choose -- has become an even larger competitor than any named rival. PMs who cannot help their sales teams overcome buyer indecision through clear positioning and differentiated value will watch deals stall indefinitely.

At the same time, the PLG revolution has raised the bar for what enterprise buyers expect: they want to try before they buy, they expect seamless onboarding, and they have zero patience for products that require months of implementation before delivering value. The most successful B2B PMs in 2026 are building products that work beautifully for individual users on day one while also meeting the governance, compliance, and integration requirements that enterprise procurement teams demand. This dual mandate -- delightful individual experience plus enterprise-grade infrastructure -- is the defining challenge of modern B2B product strategy.

## Key Insights from Lenny's Newsletter & Podcast

1. **In B2B, product-market fit is never a binary moment -- it is a continual process.** Shishir Mehrotra (CEO of Coda) describes it perfectly: "You always have product-market fit with one group and not with the next. The group that you haven't achieved product-market fit with is the one you really want to target." Even at YouTube with 100M DAUs, they never felt they had PMF because they had teenagers but not adults. Each new segment requires re-finding fit.
   *Source: a-guide-for-finding-product-market-fit-in-b2b.md*

2. **40-60% of B2B purchase processes end in "no decision" -- and it is not because the status quo won.** April Dunford's research (via Matthew Dixon's analysis of 2M+ sales calls) reveals that buyers fail to buy because they cannot figure out how to confidently make a decision. They are more worried about messing up than missing out. Your real competition is the buyer's fear of making the wrong choice.
   *Source: how-to-build-a-killer-sales-pitch.md*

3. **A better sales pitch teaches the buyer about the whole market, not just your product.** Dunford's pitch structure has two parts: the Setup (market insight, alternative approaches with pros/cons, the "perfect world") and the Follow-through (differentiated value and proof). The setup should take only a few minutes but is the crucial context that helps buyers understand why your differentiated value matters. If the prospect agrees with your "perfect world" description, the sale is yours.
   *Source: how-to-build-a-killer-sales-pitch.md*

4. **The median time from idea to feeling product-market fit in B2B is roughly 2 years.** Start to worry if you have been working for over 2 years without feeling PMF and seriously worry after 3 years. Most companies got an alpha product out the door in 1-3 months. From a working product to feeling PMF typically took 9-18 months.
   *Source: a-guide-for-finding-product-market-fit-in-b2b.md*

5. **100% of the companies studied moved upmarket over time.** Every one of the 30 B2B companies Lenny analyzed moved upmarket. Almost everyone started with VSBs or SMBs. Brian Balfour explains: "Starting downmarket requires you to focus on simplicity. It is easier to add things to adapt to enterprise than to start complex and simplify over time."
   *Source: gtm-motions-of-30-b2b-saas-companies.md*

6. **Your first 10 B2B customers come from hand-to-hand combat, not scalable channels.** None of the seven strategies that founders used to find early customers (personal network, cold outbound, investor networks, communities, content, press, just launching) are scalable. Trust is the key -- and the most common path is through former colleagues and friends of early employees.
   *Source: how-to-find-and-win-your-first-10-b2b-customers.md*

7. **Everyone targets one (and max three) personas within an organization.** Amplitude targeted mobile PMs and growth leads. Retool targeted internal tools engineers. Front targeted ops, sales, and support leads. The more focused you can get, the easier it is to nail positioning, outreach, and product strategy.
   *Source: gtm-motions-of-30-b2b-saas-companies.md*

8. **Retool used Crunchbase to find early customers with surgical precision.** They filtered by last fundraise date and vertical (avoiding SaaS, targeting operationally heavy businesses like delivery and fintech), then emailed the CTO and VP of Operations at each company -- because engineering feels the pain of not building tools, while operations feels the pain of not having them. This is how they landed DoorDash, Rappi, and Brex.
   *Source: how-to-find-and-win-your-first-10-b2b-customers.md*

## How to Build This Skill

**Foundations:**
- Define your ICP with precision. Go beyond "mid-market tech companies" to specify the role, the pain point, the buying process, and the firmographic attributes that predict success.
- Study the B2B buying process from the buyer's perspective. Read April Dunford's framework on how buying is harder than selling -- understand the fear, the stakeholder dynamics, and why "no decision" wins so often.
- Map your product's current position on the PLG-to-SLG spectrum. Understand which customers can self-serve, which need sales assistance, and which require full enterprise sales cycles.

**Intermediate practices:**
- Build your sales pitch using Dunford's structure: Start with your market insight (your unique point of view), walk through alternative approaches with honest pros and cons, describe the "perfect world," then prove you deliver it. Never start with a feature walkthrough.
- Implement a product-qualified lead system that identifies accounts showing enterprise buying signals: multiple users from the same company, usage of collaboration features, hitting usage limits, or visiting the pricing page.
- Create an enterprise readiness roadmap: SSO, SAML, audit logs, role-based access controls, SOC 2 compliance, data residency options. Prioritize based on which enterprise requirements are blocking the most revenue.

**Advanced practices:**
- Design your land-and-expand motion. Define the "land" (how an individual or small team starts using the product) and the "expand" triggers (what signals that an account is ready for upsell to a team, department, or company-wide plan).
- Build a structured founder-led sales process. As a PM, you should be joining sales calls regularly to understand buyer objections, competitive dynamics, and unmet needs. Use Gong-style call recording to analyze patterns across dozens of calls.
- Develop a competitive intelligence practice. Track how competitors position themselves, what they make free vs. paid, how they pitch against you, and what enterprise features they prioritize. Feed this into your positioning and roadmap.

## Self-Assessment Questions

1. Can you articulate your product's differentiated value in one sentence -- not what it does, but what value it uniquely delivers that no alternative can?
2. Do you know the typical buying process for your product -- who initiates, who evaluates, who has budget authority, and what can cause a deal to stall or die?
3. Have you built a sales pitch that teaches buyers about the whole market (not just your product) and helps them confidently make a decision?
4. Can you identify the top three enterprise requirements blocking expansion into larger accounts, and do you have a plan to address them?
5. Are you systematically capturing and analyzing buyer objections from sales calls to inform your product roadmap?
