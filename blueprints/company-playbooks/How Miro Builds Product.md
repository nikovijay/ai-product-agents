# How Miro Builds Product

> Company Playbook | Source: Lenny's Newsletter & Podcast

## Company Overview

Miro is a collaborative visual workspace used by over 50 million users, valued at $17.5 billion (one of the 10 most valuable U.S. startups at time of profiling). Its product organization of ~700 people (called AMPED) is notable for its structured approach to OKRs, multi-stage product reviews, codified product principles, and a "painted picture" vision process. Under CPO Varun Parmar, Miro balances operational rigor with creative energy, maintaining a strong emphasis on shipping customer value fast with high quality.

## Org Structure

- **AMPED cross-functional teams:** The product org is composed of Analytics, (Product) Marketing, Product, Engineering, and Design -- hence AMPED. Product Marketing is embedded from day one, not bolted on at launch.
- **Seven product streams:**
  1. Core product experience
  2. Enterprise
  3. Growth
  4. Core product foundation
  5. Infrastructure
  6. Platform
  7. Data
- **50+ PMs** within the ~700-person AMPED org.
- **Stream-based ownership:** Each stream focuses on specific user personas or outcomes (e.g., Enterprise stream owns admin/security persona; Growth is horizontal, focusing on acquisition and activation).
- **Common metrics across streams:** Outcomes like improving first-time user experience are shared responsibilities supported by multiple streams.
- **Geographically, the product org is based in Europe;** GTM teams are global.

## Planning Process

- **Three-year "painted picture":** A bold, high-level vision co-developed by Miro's Leadership Team defining desired outcomes for users and the company. This provides directional alignment across the entire organization.
- **Annual strategy (November-January, three steps):**
  1. Product Leadership identifies priorities and an initial list of initiatives (continuing, stopping, or adding).
  2. AMPED leaders refine priorities at an in-person offsite (4-5 days), make tradeoff decisions, and begin staffing discussions.
  3. The full 700+ AMPED org reviews the strategy at an all-hands, with Q&A sessions (1,000+ questions in the most recent iteration).
- **Rolling six-month roadmap:** 80% confidence in the current quarter, 50%+ in the next. Mid-year strategy revision based on progress and market context.
- **Semi-annual OKRs (evolved from quarterly):** Laddered from Company to AMPED to Stream level. Teams decide which OKRs they support rather than creating their own at every level. Monthly traction meetings review progress; quarterly check-ins allow course correction.
- **Resource allocation rule of thumb:** 60% product innovation, 20% running the business (maintenance/bugs), 20% tech innovation (reducing tech debt). Adjusted by product maturity.

## How They Execute

- **Multi-stage product reviews (P-Strat, P0, P1, P2):**
  - **P-Strat:** Long-term strategy and vision.
  - **P0:** Opportunity and problem identification.
  - **P1:** Proposed solution.
  - **P2:** Post-launch performance assessment.
- **~5 hours/week of sync product review meetings** for large initiatives. Cross-functional AMPED teams present together (not just PMs). Reviews are open to all but critical stakeholders are specifically identified and ensured.
- **Async pre-reads via Talktrack:** PMs send board recordings days in advance. Reviewers add comments/questions directly on the board. Meetings skip presentations and jump to discussion.
- **Small/medium projects approved via Slack.**
- **Decisions published in Slack** after meetings so the full org can see what was decided and why.
- **Autonomous team execution:** Teams choose their own estimation techniques (planning poker, T-shirt sizing, etc.) and ways of working. No enforced common system for delivery tracking.
- **Tooling:** Jira for task management and bugs; Slack for async messaging; Miro boards throughout the product development lifecycle (design sprints, reviews, retros, user flow mapping); Coda, Confluence, and Google Docs for documentation.

## What Makes Them Distinctive

1. **The "Mona Lisa" principle:** Everything shipped should be something the team is proud to put their name on, like a masterpiece painting. Backed by monthly design reviews where the Design team evaluates everything shipped and calls out what meets (or misses) the quality bar with real examples.
2. **Eight codified product principles** that guide prioritization and design decisions, including "Best-in-class collaboration before solo scenarios," "Evidence over opinions," and "Fast learning to get PMF before scaling."
3. **Three-year painted picture vision** that gives the entire company directional alignment beyond quarterly cycles, balancing long-term ambition with short-term agility.
4. **AMPED structure with embedded Product Marketing:** Having marketing embedded in the product development process from the start ensures teams think about positioning and differentiation while building, not after.
5. **Miro Connect showcase ritual:** A monthly, company-wide event (first Friday of each month) where 140+ teams present their work. Started as a small product org experiment; became the company's most popular knowledge-sharing ritual.

## Key Quotes & Insights

1. "Deliver customer value faster with high quality." -- The Product team's motto, where every word was deliberately defined and debated by Product Leadership.
2. "Everything we ship should be like a Mona Lisa painting, something we'd be proud of putting our name on." -- The Mona Lisa principle for quality.
3. "When changes are happening in the market or inside the company, you should be agile and ready to adjust, not trapped by your own planning." -- On flexible roadmapping, citing the rapid 8-week Miro AI beta as an example.
4. "Having OKRs at every level meant each team had to define their objectives and initiatives. This often resulted in teams rephrasing the same initiative at different altitudes." -- On why Miro removed extra OKR layers.
5. "We were planning one month every quarter -- that's 33% of the time" -- is a paraphrase, but captures the pain of over-indexing on process. (Note: This specific quote is from Ramp's Geoff Charles, but Miro experienced a similar problem and moved from quarterly to semi-annual OKRs for the same reason.)
6. "A picture is worth a thousand words -- similarly, real examples of high quality are a lot more powerful, and simple at the same time." -- On why monthly design reviews with real shipped examples beat abstract quality guidelines.
7. "Product Marketing is part of our product development process from the start. So we're not only thinking of what to build but how to position it, how to differentiate, and how to reach the users." -- On the AMPED structure.

## What You Can Steal

1. **Create a "painted picture" 3-year vision.** Co-develop a bold, high-level document describing what success looks like for users and the business in three years. Use it to anchor annual strategy and prevent quarter-to-quarter tunnel vision.
2. **Run monthly design reviews on shipped work.** Have the design team review everything that shipped each month and publicly highlight what meets or misses the quality bar using real examples -- far more effective than abstract quality guidelines.
3. **Embed Product Marketing in the product team from day one.** Do not treat positioning and differentiation as a post-build activity. Include marketing perspectives in problem definition and solution design.
4. **Use Talktrack-style async pre-reads.** Record walkthroughs of your review material and share them days before meetings. Let reviewers add comments asynchronously so live meetings can skip presentations and focus on decisions.
5. **Ladder OKRs to avoid duplication.** Set OKRs at no more than 3 levels (company, department, stream/team). Have individual teams choose which higher-level OKRs they support rather than inventing their own objectives at every layer.
