## Case Study / Project Prompts

### Slack (Growth PM, all levels) -- Merci Grace
**Prompt:** "Every day, human beings create new Slack teams. Go through the process of creating a new Slack team. Assume you have reasonable intent. Identify issues or missed opportunities with the new-user experience that hold people back from having meaningful conversations. Generate experiment ideas, rank them, and flesh out at least 3."
**What to look for:** Structure and communication clarity; curiosity (questions they ask); creativity within product constraints; gut calls when data is limited; prioritising quality customer experience
**Format:** Take-home, 72 hours
**Source:** `how-to-interview-product-managers.md`

### Chime (Identity PM, mid-senior) -- David Cheng
**Prompt:** "Imagine you're the product lead for identity at Net(Income)Flix. The CEO wants to maximise financial returns by shutting down rogue shared accounts. Come up with a plan."
**What to look for:** Answered all sub-questions; spent at least 1/4 of time on user experience; understood identity domain; success metrics tied to problem; user personas tied to features; [Bonus] pushed back that some fraud is OK to optimise UX; [Bonus] saw market opportunity in "stolen" accounts
**Format:** Take-home, 3-4 hours, 45-min presentation
**Source:** `how-to-interview-product-managers.md`

### Square (Training product PM, mid-senior) -- Kevin Yien
**Prompt:** "You are the PM for Square Training, a new product to help merchants train staff on Square products. Prepare a short presentation on what you would build, in what order, and why."
**What to look for:** Problem context grounded in actual customer conversations; tangible solution defined enough for real discussion; before/during/after thinking; design + technical + GTM constraints; ability to improve upon the idea live in the meeting
**Format:** Take-home, 2-3 days (expected 2-3 hours), 30-min presentation + 15-min discussion
**Source:** `how-to-interview-product-managers.md`

### Airbnb (Host-side PM, IC and managers) -- Lenny Rachitsky
**Prompt:** "When guests attempt to book, 50% of the time they are ignored or rejected by hosts. How would you increase this conversion rate from 50% to 90%?" (With context: rejections are mostly logistical; hosts sometimes miss requests; 24-hour response window.)
**What to look for:** How they broke down the opportunity; which lever they see as biggest and why; big thinking vs. incremental optimisation; prioritisation logic; clarity of proposal; balance of both marketplace sides
**Format:** 90 minutes, day-of interview
**Source:** `how-to-interview-product-managers.md`

### Procore/AppFolio (PM, general) -- Ronnie Regev
**Prompt:** "Pick a modern collaboration tool and design a new feature or improvements to acquisition/activation/engagement. Constraints: 3 months dev time, team of 4, present problem + customer value + solution + success criteria + roadmap + trade-offs + release strategy + mid-fidelity mockup + one-pager."
**What to look for:** Product sense and execution; visual (mocks), verbal (presentation), and prose (one-pager) articulation
**Format:** Take-home, few hours, 30-min content + 30-min discussion
**Source:** `how-to-interview-product-managers.md`

### PostHog (PM) -- James Hawkins
**Prompt:** Realistic day-to-day PM task, representative of actual work but not shippable. Deliberately too much for one person to complete in a day, to test prioritisation.
**Format:** SuperDay (1-3 days), includes coffee chats with team
**Source:** `adding-a-work-trial-to-your-interview-process.md`

## Evaluation Rubrics & Hiring Processes

### Lenny Rachitsky's 6 Core PM Interview Skills
**Process:** Interview for 6 skills: Communication, Collaboration, Execution, Strategy, Impact, Product Sense. Split across 3-6 interviewers in a full-day loop. Each interviewer tests 1-2 skills with predefined good/bad answers.
**Key insight:** "Product sense isn't ever on PM career ladders, but it is something companies interview for."
**Funnel benchmark:** You need to talk to an average of 23 PM candidates to hire one great one (data from Ashby across 75,000+ applications).
**Source:** `how-to-interview-product-managers.md`

### Lenny's Recommended Interview Structure
**Process:**
1. Recruiter phone screen (30 min) -- 50% pass rate
2. Hiring manager phone screen (30 min) -- 40-50% pass rate
3. Full-day interview (4-5 hours): project presentation + 3-6 1:1 skill interviews
4. Post-interview panel: blind vote (Strong No / No / Yes / Strong Yes), discuss, hiring manager decides
5. Reference checks (including backchannel)
6. Make a compelling offer

**Key insight:** Keep questions consistent with predefined rubrics across all candidates to avoid bias.
**Source:** `how-to-interview-product-managers.md`

### Amazon Bar Raiser Process -- Bill Carr
**Process:** Every interview loop includes one Bar Raiser -- a person outside the hiring chain who runs the debrief meeting, has technical veto power (rarely used), and ensures adherence to objective criteria (leadership principles) and behavioural interviewing methodology.
**Key insight:** Created because "we had new people hiring new people hiring new people" during hyper-growth. The Bar Raiser combats urgency bias: hiring managers want to fill roles fast; the Bar Raiser ensures they fill them with people who meet culture and excellence standards. Time commitment: up to 10 hours/week. Great leadership development opportunity for earlier-career employees.
**Source:** `bill-carr.md`

### Shishir Mehrotra's PSHE Framework (Coda)
**Process:** Evaluates all roles on four dimensions: Product sense (P), Execution (S=Shipping? actually PSHE = Product thinking, Solutions, How/Habits, Execution). Reference checks are valued above interview signals. Candidate presents at the start of the loop (both free-form "brag session" + directed prompt).
**Key insight:** "If I had to stack rank what is the best signal: reference check is top of the list. Those people worked with this person for years. What you get out of 30 minutes of artificial scenarios is never going to compare." Interview types: home court (your product), away court (their past work), neutral court (novel problems like the teleporter question). Most interviews should be neutral court.
**Source:** `shishir-mehrotra.md`

### PostHog Hiring Process -- James Hawkins
**Process:**
1. Culture interview/screening with recruiter (30 min)
2. Technical interview with team leader + 1-2 members (60 min)
3. Bar-raiser with co-founder (30 min)
4. SuperDay work trial (1-3 days, split across days if needed)

**Key insight:** "Culture fit is a huge deal. A majority of soft yeses is actually a no (unless there's a passionate advocate), because hiring the wrong person is more expensive than passing on someone who might have been good."
**Source:** `adding-a-work-trial-to-your-interview-process.md`

### Linear Work Trial Process -- Karri Saarinen
**Process:** After recruiter call, hiring manager interview, and skills assessments, candidates do a 3-5 day paid work trial ($600/day). They co-work with future teammates in the actual codebase with full tool access (GitHub, Figma, Slack, Notion). Includes kickoff, check-ins, and final presentation. Feedback submitted blind before group debrief. Anything other than a strong yes is a no.
**Key insight:** "A conventional interview process doesn't account for whether someone is truly a builder, has good taste and judgment, can take initiative, and approaches problems productively." The work trial frequently surprises -- performance differs significantly from interview impressions.
**Source:** `adding-a-work-trial-to-your-interview-process.md`

### Gumroad Work Trial -- Sahil Lavingia
**Process:** One 30-min culture fit interview, then 4-6 weeks paid contractor trial (10-20 hrs/week at $150-200/hr). Candidates get full access to internal tools and ship real features (bug fixes, sometimes significant features like wishlists).
**Key insight:** "It's so very hard a priori to know if someone can do the job. So we just pay them to do it!" Roughly 20-30% of trialers pass. "If there's any doubt, there's no doubt."
**Source:** `adding-a-work-trial-to-your-interview-process.md`

### 37signals Work Trial -- Jason Fried
**Process:** Take-home project over 7 days, paid $1,500. Prompt is intentionally vague (e.g., "Here's a screen in Basecamp, what would you do to make it better?"). No questions allowed during the project. For designers: must build the real thing (HTML/CSS), not just Figma mockups. Evaluated via presentation call where they "poke holes" and riff together.
**Key insight:** "What we're trying to find is, we want to get people on a riffing flow. This is really about how you think and approach the work. I'm only going to judge you on this project, because this is what an actual work assignment would look like here."
**Source:** `adding-a-work-trial-to-your-interview-process.md`

### Automattic Work Trial -- Matt Mullenweg
**Process:** 40-hour work trial measured in hours (not days). Candidates work on synthetic projects that closely simulate real work. Set up on Slack and P2. Evaluation via structured scorecards assessing core skills, communication, and response to feedback.
**Key insight:** "There is no substitute for working alongside someone in the trenches. We recognised we were being influenced by aspects of an interview that have no bearing on how a candidate will actually perform. Some people are amazing interviewees and charm everyone they talk to."
**Source:** `adding-a-work-trial-to-your-interview-process.md`

### Ben Erez's Product Sense Interview Framework
**Process:** 5-step structure for 35 minutes of working time: (1) Assumptions + game plan, (2) Product motivation, (3) Segmentation, (4) Problem identification, (5) Solution development. Interviewer evaluates: clear communication, product motivation, segmentation, problem identification, solution development. Excellence in one area cannot compensate for weakness in another.
**Key insight:** "The best candidates approach these interviews as a specific game with clear rules rather than a casual conversation." State assumptions, outline game plan, and get interviewer buy-in before diving in.
**Source:** `the-definitive-guide-to-mastering-product-sense-interviews.md`

### Ben Erez's Analytical Thinking Interview Framework
**Process:** 5-step structure: (1) Assumptions + game plan, (2) Product rationale, (3) Metric framework (ecosystem value, metric definition, NSM + guardrails), (4) Goal-setting (altitude shift to team-level), (5) Tradeoff evaluation. Allocate most time to metric framework.
**Key insight:** "If you can't describe your metric to a data scientist in a way that they could run a query with, it's not a useful metric." Avoid averages/ratios as North Star metrics. Be decisive -- state your decision rather than hedging.
**Source:** `the-definitive-guide-to-mastering-analytical-thinking-interviews.md`

### Coach Erika's MVIP (Minimum Viable Interview Prep)
**Process:** (1) Get your career story straight, (2) Walk down memory lane (recall 3-5 major projects in detail), (3) Master behavioural frameworks (STAR++ for pure behavioural; outline-then-example for theoretical; clarify-assume-plan for situational), (4) Formulate high-signal questions for interviewers.
**Key insight:** "Pick 3-5 recent major projects and remember every single detail about them" rather than writing answers to hundreds of questions. Use STAR++ (add learnings and future improvements to standard STAR). 70% of behavioural questions are "pure" (tell me about a time...), 20% theoretical, 10% situational.
**Source:** `how-to-pass-any-first-round-interview-even-in-a-terrible-talent-market.md`

## Early-Stage Hiring Insights

### What Makes Startups Great at Hiring (5 Themes)
1. **Captivating vision:** "Articulating a crystal clear and ambitious vision makes it easy for candidates to visualise what they are building towards." -- Kevin Tan, Snackpass
2. **A++ early team:** "Great people want to work with great people. Hiring definitely compounds." -- Julianna Lamb, Stytch
3. **Putting in the work:** "Be prepared to commit ~100 hours for each hire you make." -- Sam Corcos, Levels
4. **Founder's network:** Most first hires come from founders' personal networks
5. **Pitching prowess:** "Treat closing a candidate almost exactly the same as closing an investor." -- Siqi Chen, Runway

**Source:** `winning-at-early-stage-hiring.md`

### When to Hire Your First PM
Hire a PM when you have 8-10 engineers. "The right time for PMs to join the company is about 8 to 10 engineers." Only 3 of 25+ companies hired a PM in their first 3 hires (Coda, Persona, Snyk). A quarter of companies hired a PM by their first 10 hires.

**Source:** `hiring-your-early-team.md`, `gokul-rajaram.md`

### Closing Tactics
- **Stytch:** Surprise Zoom with entire interview panel sharing why they're excited about the candidate
- **Runway:** "Our pitch and demo for our Series A is pretty much exactly the pitch and demo we do for candidates"
- **Vori:** Custom economic outcomes Notion doc walking through market structure, gradient of potential outcomes, and comp comparison -- plus candor about risks

**Source:** `winning-at-early-stage-hiring.md`
