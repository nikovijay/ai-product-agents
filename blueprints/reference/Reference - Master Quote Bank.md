## Product Strategy

> "People don't buy the problem, they buy your solution. Obviously they don't buy it if it's not solving something they care about, but there are many products solving what they care about. The real question is, do you solve it better than everybody else so that they buy you?"
> — **Marty Cagan**, Founder, Silicon Valley Product Group | Source: `marty-cagan.md`

> "Most execution problems are actually strategy problems in disguise."
> — **Shreyas Doshi**, Former PM Leader at Stripe, Twitter, Google | Source: `shreyas-doshi.md`

> "A strategy is a design for overcoming a high-stakes challenge. Don't call it strategy — call it an action agenda. Begin to identify the one or two key challenges that can actually be addressed and what are we going to do about it."
> — **Richard Rumelt**, Author of *Good Strategy Bad Strategy* | Source: `richard-rumelt.md`

> "Bad strategy is mistaking goals for strategy. Saying 'we're not growing fast enough' is not a diagnosis — it's a statement of value. You'd like to grow faster. I'd like to be taller."
> — **Richard Rumelt**, Author of *Good Strategy Bad Strategy* | Source: `richard-rumelt.md`

> "Delight customers in hard-to-copy, margin-enhancing ways. That's it. That's product strategy."
> — **Gibson Biddle**, Former VP Product, Netflix | Source: `gibson-biddle.md`

> "Focus is the fundamental source of power in strategy. Trying to do too many different things is defocusing."
> — **Richard Rumelt**, Author of *Good Strategy Bad Strategy* | Source: `richard-rumelt.md`

> "Column A is looking at the bricks around you and stacking them. Column B is imagining the magical future you want, then working backwards. Most planning is Column A. The companies that change the world are Column B."
> — **Melanie Perkins**, CEO & Co-Founder, Canva | Source: `melanie-perkins.md`

> "The thing I love about a crazy big goal is that you feel completely inadequate before it. You want to work really hard to will it into existence."
> — **Melanie Perkins**, CEO & Co-Founder, Canva | Source: `melanie-perkins.md`

> "Tension is at the heart of every art form and every innovation. When we launch a new product, we say it can do X — and the person starts imagining what their life might be like if that were true. If they fall in love with that possibility, now there's tension."
> — **Seth Godin**, Author & Entrepreneur | Source: `seth-godin.md`

> "AI very soon is going to stop being a feature the same way electricity is not a feature. The question is: what's in this for the user? What promise do I want to make — a difficult promise, a remarkable promise — and then how do I keep it?"
> — **Seth Godin**, Author & Entrepreneur | Source: `seth-godin.md`

> "Power requires a benefit and a barrier. You're on a treadmill — if you stop running, you get creamed — but that's not power. The things that drive operational excellence can be mimicked."
> — **Hamilton Helmer**, Author of *7 Powers* | Source: `hamilton-helmer.md`

> "Almost every startup starts with counter-positioning. Your competition is functional competition. If you don't have counter-positioning, you're at high risk from an incumbent who just has to extend their product."
> — **Hamilton Helmer**, Author of *7 Powers* | Source: `hamilton-helmer.md`

> "When you combine high energy, sound judgment, low ego, and small teams — you just get magic."
> — **Shreyas Doshi**, Former PM Leader at Stripe, Twitter, Google | Source: `shreyas-doshi.md`

## Growth & Retention

> "Building a great product is necessary but not sufficient. The separation is between those that build really great distribution."
> — **Brian Balfour**, Founder & CEO, Reforge | Source: `brian-balfour.md`

> "The most powerful technology shifts are the ones that come with new distribution platforms. We had internet, cloud, mobile, social — and the most impactful ones always brought new ways to distribute products."
> — **Brian Balfour**, Founder & CEO, Reforge | Source: `brian-balfour.md`

> "There is no such thing as a truly viral product. The true secret behind virality is word of mouth — the virality you can't measure, that isn't a mechanic, that isn't in a feature. It's when one user spontaneously tells another user about your product."
> — **Rahul Vohra**, Founder & CEO, Superhuman | Source: `rahul-vohra.md`

> "You have to deliberately not act on the feedback of many of your early users. We're here to make something people want, but it can't be all people."
> — **Rahul Vohra**, Founder & CEO, Superhuman | Source: `rahul-vohra.md`

> "How would you feel if you could no longer use this product? Once you get a high enough percentage saying 'very disappointed,' most of those products did pretty well. If it's too low, those products tended to suffer."
> — **Sean Ellis**, Author of *Hacking Growth*, Coined "Growth Hacking" | Source: `sean-ellis.md`

> "Ignore the people who say they'd be 'somewhat disappointed.' They're telling you it's a nice-to-have. If you start tweaking based on their feedback, you might dilute it for your must-have users."
> — **Sean Ellis**, Author of *Hacking Growth* | Source: `sean-ellis.md`

> "For every social app I've ever built, the number of invitations sent per user drops 20% for every additional year of age from 13 to 18. If you build for adults, expect to acquire every user with ads."
> — **Nikita Bier**, Co-Founder of TBH & Gas | Source: `nikita-bier.md`

> "Every tap on a mobile app is a miracle for you as a product developer. Users switch between apps at a high frequency. Every single tap is so scarce that you should be optimizing everything."
> — **Nikita Bier**, Co-Founder of TBH & Gas | Source: `nikita-bier.md`

> "To figure out your product-market fit and how to distribute it — that's not something you can outsource to somebody. The first wave of growth has to be founder-led."
> — **Elena Verna**, Growth Leader, Miro / Amplitude / SurveyMonkey | Source: `elena-verna-30.md`

> "I think the most interesting markets, you have to think of them like currents — something happening that creates momentum where a plank of wood on the river gets pulled forward. What you're looking for are the dynamics of change."
> — **Sarah Tavel**, Partner, Benchmark; Former First PM at Pinterest | Source: `sarah-tavel.md`

> "The test for whether you're building a retentive product: it should get better the more you use it, and you should have more to lose by leaving it."
> — **Sarah Tavel**, Partner, Benchmark | Source: `sarah-tavel.md`

> "If you don't build the network effect into what you're making, you are almost certainly going to fail. The question is: will this work better for my users if they tell other people about it? And if the answer is no, then why would they?"
> — **Seth Godin**, Author & Entrepreneur | Source: `seth-godin.md`

## Discovery & Customer Insight

> "People hire products, they don't buy them. They hire them to make progress in their life."
> — **Bob Moesta**, Co-Creator of Jobs to Be Done | Source: `bob-moesta.md`

> "Snickers competes with a protein drink. Milky Way competes with a glass of wine. Jobs to Be Done helps you see the true competitive set from the demand side — not the supply side."
> — **Bob Moesta**, Co-Creator of Jobs to Be Done | Source: `bob-moesta.md`

> "It's not just about pain and gain — it's about context and outcome. When you hear a customer story and it seems irrational, nine times out of ten it's because you don't have the rest of the story. The context makes the irrational rational."
> — **Bob Moesta**, Co-Creator of Jobs to Be Done | Source: `bob-moesta.md`

> "I think the heart of good product is really getting comfortable in the problem space — really taking the time to frame a problem well before we jump to solutions. But it's the opposite of how our brains are wired."
> — **Teresa Torres**, Author of *Continuous Discovery Habits* | Source: `teresa-torres.md`

> "I can tell you that 98% of people who write opportunities write them as solutions. We just really struggle with the distinction between the problem space and the solution space."
> — **Teresa Torres**, Author of *Continuous Discovery Habits* | Source: `teresa-torres.md`

> "Fall in love with the problem, not the solution. The simplest way to create value is to solve a problem."
> — **Uri Levine**, Co-Founder of Waze | Source: `uri-levine.md`

> "The biggest mistake teams make is they become passionate about a solution to a problem instead of developing empathy for the customer suffering the problem. The empathy gives you the solution."
> — **Scott Belsky**, CPO, Adobe; Founder, Behance | Source: `scott-belsky.md`

> "If you do a pre-mortem right, you will not have to do an ugly post-mortem. Imagine this project has miserably failed six months from now. Now work backwards — what went wrong?"
> — **Shreyas Doshi**, Former PM Leader at Stripe, Twitter, Google | Source: `shreyas-doshi.md`

> "Positioning is foundational. When we get it wrong, everything sucks. When we get it right, we all make money."
> — **April Dunford**, Author of *Obviously Awesome* | Source: `april-dunford.md`

> "A struggling moment causes demand — not supply. Look at where people are trying to obtain a particular value and going through a very distortive process. If you can crystallize what their motivation is, you can have intense adoption."
> — **Nikita Bier**, Co-Founder of TBH & Gas | Source: `nikita-bier.md`

## Execution & Shipping

> "If you're not sure who the decision maker is, it's probably you. I'd rather you act that way than slow the whole company down. Be a force for positive momentum — it will be a real career maker."
> — **Claire Hughes Johnson**, Former COO, Stripe | Source: `claire-hughes-johnson.md`

> "Product-market fit is just the product, and that is not a company, and that will not scale. Companies that fall over — often it's not the product, it's that they didn't build the company very well."
> — **Claire Hughes Johnson**, Former COO, Stripe | Source: `claire-hughes-johnson.md`

> "We build products with two people at a time. One programmer, one designer. Maximum six weeks. We don't get stuck in huge projects with 18-person meetings, slow decisions, and complexity."
> — **Jason Fried**, Co-Founder & CEO, 37signals | Source: `jason-fried.md`

> "Silicon Valley has found a way to make the most profitable style of business the least profitable. Software margins should be 80-90%. Most companies are barely breaking even. The corner store pizza shop would kill for these economics."
> — **Jason Fried**, Co-Founder & CEO, 37signals | Source: `jason-fried.md`

> "Products live and die in the pixels. You should be designing the hierarchy, the pixels, the flows, everything. That's on you."
> — **Nikita Bier**, Co-Founder of TBH & Gas | Source: `nikita-bier.md`

> "The advantage a startup has is not velocity — bigger companies can always get more done. The advantage is latency. You can have an idea one day and test it the next day. That turning radius is what matters."
> — **Ravi Mehta**, Former CPO, Tinder; Founder, Outpace | Source: `ravi-mehta.md`

> "Once I realize something might not be the right thing to work on, I'm either incorrect — at which point it's super important I understand why — or I'm correct — at which point it's super unfair to let people keep working on something that isn't going to make it."
> — **Tobi Lutke**, Founder & CEO, Shopify | Source: `tobi-lutke.md`

> "The overlap of most valuable things you can do with a product and things that happen to be fully quantifiable is maybe 20% — which leaves 80% of the value space unaddressable by people who will only look at quantifiable things."
> — **Tobi Lutke**, Founder & CEO, Shopify | Source: `tobi-lutke.md`

> "Goodhart's law is real. The moment a metric becomes a goal, it's no longer a useful metric. No metric by itself is a complete heuristic for a complex business."
> — **Tobi Lutke**, Founder & CEO, Shopify | Source: `tobi-lutke.md`

> "A professional doesn't ask for an extension. Things you didn't expect are going to happen — that's part of the deal."
> — **Seth Godin**, Author & Entrepreneur | Source: `seth-godin.md`

> "The most important thing is to develop a reproducible testing process. It's so unpredictable whether a consumer product idea will work. Focusing on your process for taking many shots at bat reduces risk more than anything."
> — **Nikita Bier**, Co-Founder of TBH & Gas | Source: `nikita-bier.md`

> "It's so incredibly necessary to take what's implicit and make it explicit. Your intuition is sometimes right — but if you don't make it explicit, you don't get to find out when it's wrong."
> — **Annie Duke**, Author of *Thinking in Bets* | Source: `annie-duke.md`

> "There is no such thing as a long feedback loop. The way you shorten it is to ask: what are the things that are correlated with the outcome I eventually desire?"
> — **Annie Duke**, Author of *Thinking in Bets* | Source: `annie-duke.md`

## Leadership & Management

> "Radical candor is what happens when you care personally and challenge directly at the same time. 90% of us make 90% of our mistakes in the ruinous empathy bucket — caring but failing to tell people what they need to hear."
> — **Kim Scott**, Author of *Radical Candor* | Source: `kim-scott.md`

> "When you say 'um' every third word, it makes you sound stupid. Sometimes the kindest thing someone can do is be that direct with you. It's the most career-changing feedback I ever received."
> — **Kim Scott**, Author of *Radical Candor* (re: her boss at Google) | Source: `kim-scott.md`

> "What could I do or stop doing that would make it easier to work with me? Don't write down my question — if you sound like Kim Scott and not like yourself, people won't believe you want the answer."
> — **Kim Scott**, Author of *Radical Candor* | Source: `kim-scott.md`

> "The biggest marker between a botched layoff and a successful layoff is: did they hear it from their manager in a one-on-one? If they heard it in an email or group chat, that's when people get really angry. It feels dehumanizing."
> — **Matt Mochary**, CEO Coach (OpenAI, Coinbase, Reddit) | Source: `matt-mochary.md`

> "When someone's in fear, they can't see reality. Their brain makes exaggerated predictions. I've made this bet hundreds of times — when you do the thing you fear, the exact opposite of what you predicted happens. I've never lost."
> — **Matt Mochary**, CEO Coach (OpenAI, Coinbase, Reddit) | Source: `matt-mochary.md`

> "Relationships don't scale, but culture does. When a leader treats their direct reports with real care, those reports treat their teams with real care. That creates a culture of caring that scales."
> — **Kim Scott**, Author of *Radical Candor* | Source: `kim-scott.md`

> "There's a thing I call selective micromanagement. If you don't feel confident in the direction your team is moving, the right answer is not to be hands-off. The right answer is to micromanage temporarily — then pull back."
> — **Ravi Mehta**, Former CPO, Tinder | Source: `ravi-mehta.md`

> "I really, really, really think there is not a single person on this planet who is even close to being at their maximum potential. Reminding people of their own potential constantly is actually a wonderful thing to do."
> — **Tobi Lutke**, Founder & CEO, Shopify | Source: `tobi-lutke.md`

> "Pessimism sounds extremely sophisticated. Optimism always sounds dumb or at least naive. The most powerful unquantifiable things in business are fun and delight."
> — **Tobi Lutke**, Founder & CEO, Shopify | Source: `tobi-lutke.md`

> "If you believe that talent is everything, then your hiring process is everything."
> — **Claire Hughes Johnson**, Former COO, Stripe | Source: `claire-hughes-johnson.md`

> "I realized how much it pays to seek out knowledge from others. I don't think I made enough phone calls in my earlier career. The Collisons taught me that — how much it pays to find the person who did that thing five years ago and ask what they learned."
> — **Claire Hughes Johnson**, Former COO, Stripe | Source: `claire-hughes-johnson.md`

> "Every time you hire someone new, mark your calendar for 30 days. Ask yourself one question: knowing what I know today, would I hire this person? If the answer is no, fire them immediately."
> — **Uri Levine**, Co-Founder of Waze | Source: `uri-levine.md`

## Career & Personal Growth

> — **Julie Zhuo**, Former VP Design, Facebook | Source: `julie-zhuo.md`

> "I approach my writing as letters to myself. This is the advice I need to give myself. Writing became hugely helpful for clarifying my thinking — and by the end of that year, I saw a huge difference in my ability to speak up in large meetings."

> "My energy source is dissatisfaction with the status quo. I think today is the dystopia of the future. It behooves us to try to build products that lead towards progress."

> — **Seth Godin**, Author & Entrepreneur | Source: `seth-godin.md`

> "Good taste is knowing what other people want just before they do. People with good taste bring something to the world that the world didn't expect but is glad to see."

> "Great careers aren't built by working linearly in your job and making sure you get promoted. They're built by knowing a lot of people, doing great work so they want you on their teams, and waiting for serendipity — then seizing it."
> — **Gokul Rajaram**, Product Leader at DoorDash; Board: Coinbase, Pinterest | Source: `gokul-rajaram.md`

> "The reason it's great for entrepreneurs to start bootstrapping is they get more practice making money — the fundamental skill you need to run a successful business."
> — **Jason Fried**, Co-Founder & CEO, 37signals | Source: `jason-fried.md`

## Frameworks & Mental Models

> "I define product management on a feature team versus a real product team as so different that they should not use the same title. On a feature team, you herd cats. On an empowered team, you discover a solution that's valuable, usable, feasible, and viable."
> — **Marty Cagan**, Founder, Silicon Valley Product Group | Source: `marty-cagan.md`

> "Steve Jobs said the idea is minor — just the start. The craftsmanship of going from idea to product — that's product discovery. Most executives think an idea is 90% of the work. They don't understand that things change constantly with every iteration."
> — **Marty Cagan**, Founder, Silicon Valley Product Group (on Steve Jobs) | Source: `marty-cagan.md`

> "When a company stops innovating, the people celebrated are marketing, sales, and finance people. Good product people don't want to work there anymore — they leave. Steve Jobs identified this disease in 1995 and it still happens all the time."
> — **Marty Cagan**, Founder, Silicon Valley Product Group (on Steve Jobs) | Source: `marty-cagan.md`

> "A startup is an experiment. A pivot is what it's called when you change the strategy but try to maintain fidelity to the vision. These things need names because we do them all the time."
> — **Eric Ries**, Creator of Lean Startup Methodology | Source: `eric-ries.md`

> "My north star metric is insights per minute. That's what I optimize for whenever I have any live meetings."
> — **Elena Verna**, Growth Leader, Miro / Amplitude / SurveyMonkey | Source: `elena-verna-30.md`

> "If you think about a roadmap — I'm literally trying to tell you what I'll build in the next 24 months, but none of us saw ChatGPT coming. If instead you talk about struggling moments, the roadmap becomes: when I get to that moment, there's multiple ways I can solve it."
> — **Bob Moesta**, Co-Creator of Jobs to Be Done | Source: `bob-moesta.md`

> "The purpose of a meeting is not discover, discuss, decide. The only thing that's supposed to happen in a meeting is the discussion part. Discovery and decision should happen before and after."
> — **Annie Duke**, Author of *Thinking in Bets* | Source: `annie-duke.md`

> "I learned marketing is the product. You don't make a product and hand it to some marketing yahoo and say, go put a logo on this. The product is why it works."
> — **Seth Godin**, Author & Entrepreneur | Source: `seth-godin.md`
